Moving from Dynamics NAV to Business Central: make your migration manageable

A move is more than a technical upgrade. Reliable planning starts by understanding your NAV environment, its customizations and the workflows people actually use.

Published 20 October 2026 · 7 min read

Dynamics NAV environments vary by version, extensions, integrations and deployment model. There is no single migration plan that fits every organization. An assessment makes dependencies visible before timelines and effort are agreed.

Start with an inventory

Record the NAV version, databases, environments, modules and the people responsible for the system. Add which teams depend on it every day and which processes are most important to operations. Also identify how requirements have changed since the original implementation.

Assess customizations and extensions

Document custom code, add-ons, reports and changes to standard functionality. For each item, ask: Is it still needed? Does standard functionality or a maintained extension cover the need? Does the existing logic need to be rebuilt? These answers help you avoid carrying forward unnecessary legacy complexity.

Include data and integrations

Decide which master and transaction data is needed at go-live and what must remain available for compliance, reference or analysis. Review quality and completeness. Also list connected systems and agree technical contacts, data flows and ways to test each connection.

Define the target and approach

The suitable target version, deployment and migration approach depend on your starting point and current Microsoft guidance. Discuss the sequence of work, the test environment and whether a coordinated or phased go-live makes sense. A pilot may be appropriate; the decision should reflect the impact on processes, data and day-to-day operations.

Test realistic workflows

Do more than confirm that data was transferred. Test the workflows employees actually perform, such as order entry, purchasing, posting, warehouse movements and reporting, as relevant to your business. Record expected results, document differences and repeat critical tests after fixes.

Support the transition into daily work

Plan data finalization, permissions, open transactions, communications and responsibilities for cutover day. Train users around their tasks rather than only around software features. Agree how questions and issues will be captured and prioritized after launch.

Questions for an initial migration discussion

  • Which NAV version and customizations are currently in use?
  • Which business processes must work reliably on day one?
  • Which data and external systems need to be migrated or connected?
  • Who will validate the results and approve go-live?
  • How will employees be trained and supported after launch?

Answering these questions makes it easier to agree a realistic migration scope. Compatibility and available upgrade paths must be checked against your specific NAV version and target environment.

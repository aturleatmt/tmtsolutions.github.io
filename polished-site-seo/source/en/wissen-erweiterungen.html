Extending Business Central: give AL customizations and integrations a clear purpose

Custom functionality can support the way your business works. To keep extensions useful over time, they should solve a defined problem and fit your update and support plans.

Published 20 October 2026 · 6 min read

Business Central provides standard features and configuration options. When these do not meet a requirement, an extension or integration may be appropriate. The important first step is to separate the business need from a technical solution that may have been assumed too early.

Describe the need first

Explain who performs the task, what information they need and what does not work today. Include exceptions and the expected outcome. A concrete process description is easier to compare with standard functionality than the statement “we need a customization.”

Standard, configuration or extension?

First check whether an existing feature or setup can meet the need. If it cannot, consider whether the process can change without compromising an important business requirement. Only then decide whether a custom AL extension is necessary. This keeps additions focused and the overall solution easier to understand.

Design AL extensions for maintainability

An extension should have a defined purpose, clear ownership and useful documentation. Agree what data it reads or changes, how errors are handled and who maintains it when requirements change. Plan tests for the business workflow and relevant updates. The technical approach must fit the Business Central version and applicable development guidelines.

Treat integrations as production systems

An integration involves more than the fields being transferred. Define which system owns each piece of information, when data is exchanged and what happens with delays, duplicates or errors. Assign responsibility for credentials, monitoring, changes and support.

Create a short decision checklist before building

  • Requirement: Which specific task should be easier or more reliable?
  • Standard review: Which built-in feature or configuration has been considered?
  • Impact: Which people, data and connected systems are affected?
  • Operations: Who will maintain, test and update the solution?
  • Acceptance: How will business owners confirm that it works?

These questions help the team assess effort and value before implementation. A small addition is not automatically simple to operate: documentation, testing and clear ownership are part of the solution.

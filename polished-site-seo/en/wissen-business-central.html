Preparing a Business Central implementation: start with a clear plan

A successful ERP implementation is not about enabling the most features. It starts with a shared understanding of what your business wants to achieve.

Published 20 October 2026 · 6 min read

Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central can bring core financial and operational processes together in one ERP environment. The right scope depends on your processes, data, existing systems and responsibilities. The preparation below helps turn an implementation into a practical project.

1. Agree on goals and scope

First, describe which specific workflows should improve or become more reliable. “Improve visibility of outstanding orders” is more useful than a long wish list with no priorities. Decide which teams and processes belong in the first phase and what can follow later.

  • Which tasks take the most time or create repeated questions today?
  • Which teams and locations are involved?
  • How will you know the solution is delivering the intended value?

2. Map the work, not just the software

Describe how a transaction moves from start to finish in practice. Include the people who do the work every day, and note exceptions and hand-offs between departments. Then compare those needs with Business Central’s standard functionality. This helps identify where configuration may be enough and where further analysis is needed.

3. Review data quality and ownership

Master data and open transactions affect how well a new system works from day one. Check for duplicate customer or vendor records, missing fields, product information and the data that needs to be migrated. Agree who approves business data and who makes decisions about setup and access.

4. Identify integrations and extensions early

List the systems that need to exchange information with Business Central, such as an online shop, warehouse solution, document management system or industry application. Record which data moves in each direction, how often it needs to move and who supports the connection. Check standard options first; a custom extension should address a clearly described requirement.

5. Plan implementation, testing and training

A project plan needs to include more than configuration and data migration. Make room for business testing, go-live preparation and user support. Test typical workflows with representative data and invite future users to give feedback. Plan time for questions after go-live as well.

A practical preparation checklist

  1. Document goals, priorities and project scope.
  2. Name process owners and decision-makers.
  3. List current systems, customizations and required integrations.
  4. Review data quality, ownership and migration scope.
  5. Plan testing, training, go-live and ongoing support.

This checklist is not a substitute for an individual requirements assessment. It gives your team a shared starting point for identifying questions and dependencies. Features, licensing and deployment should be checked against your specific requirements and Business Central version.

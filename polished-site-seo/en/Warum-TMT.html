ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLES

Two common ERP scenarios.

The following scenarios are fictional. They are not real TMT projects, customer references or promised results.

FICTIONAL EXAMPLE · WHOLESALE

Bringing order and inventory information together

Starting situation
A hypothetical mid-sized wholesaler manages order and inventory information across several tools. Employees often reconcile data manually.
Possible approach
Map processes and data ownership, assess Business Central standard functionality, and agree an appropriate scope for sales, purchasing, inventory and data migration.
Possible objective
Bring key order and inventory information into agreed workflows. Actual benefits depend on requirements, data quality and implementation.

FICTIONAL EXAMPLE · MANUFACTURING

Connecting ERP and production systems in a coordinated way

Starting situation
A hypothetical manufacturer transfers order data between its ERP and production system partly by hand. Responsibilities for errors and corrections are not clearly defined.
Possible approach
Define data flows and systems of record, assess standard options first, and plan an integration or AL extension only where a documented need exists.
Possible objective
Reduce duplicate entry and make transfer errors easier to trace. This is a possible project objective, not a guaranteed result.
